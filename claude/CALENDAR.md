# How "Ticks" are Displayed

The calendar is displayed using a modification of the Tzolk'in and Haab' systems of the Maya civilization. The Tzolk'in consists of a 260-day cycle with 20 day names and 13 numbers, while the Modified Haab' is a "tick calendar" with 20 months of 20 days each, 400 day years, and 8000 day cycles.

The Tzolk'in date is represented by a combination of a number (1 to 13) and a day name (one of the 20 day names). 

The Haab' date is represented by a "day" number (0 to 19), a dot, a "month" number, a dot, a "year" number, a dot, and a "cycle" number.  e.g. 13.2.7.12 is 13 cycle, 2 year, 7 month, 12 day.

In this context, a "day" is actually one "tick" of the simulation.

## NOTE

A "day" isn't really a day in the conventional sense; it represents one "tick" of the simulation, which is significantly longer than an actual day -- I estimate it will be close to 3 weeks per "tick".
